User guide

Navwithin Demo

An interactive 2D and 3D map of a hospital. A visitor searches for a place, sees the route, and can walk it on screen or carry it on a phone. This guide explains every control, who it helps, and how to use it.

Quick start

Reach any room in three steps

The same three steps work on a desktop, a phone and a lobby touch screen.

Search or tap

Type a department, a doctor or a room code such as ED-02. You can also tap a room on the map or pick one from the directory.

Navigate

Press Navigate Here. The route appears with distance, walking time, and written directions. Switch on step-free to avoid stairs.

Walk it

Press play for a first-person walkthrough. Or scan the QR code to carry the route on your phone.

Every control, one by one

Each icon below is the same one you see in the app.

Search and directory

Search box

Finds departments, doctors, symptoms, appointment terms and room codes in English or Hindi. Results rank the best match first and name the doctor when one matches.

Category chips

Filter the map and the list by type. Rooms outside the chosen type fade, so the ones you want stand out.

Quick demo routes

One press loads a ready-made journey: Cardiology Clinic (1A) across two floors, MRI & CT Imaging on the ground floor, and Visit Willow Ward (L2) to compare lift against stairs. Three more are in Demo Data.

Directory

A list of the places on the floor you are viewing, each with its room code. Tap one to open its card and fly the camera to it.

Minimize

Folds the search card down to one line so the map has the whole screen. The chevron next to the name opens it again.

Floors and views

G

G, L1, L2

G holds emergency, imaging, lab, pharmacy and the main reception. L1 holds outpatient clinics and rehab. L2 holds surgery, intensive care and the inpatient wards. A small dot on a floor button means your route touches that floor.

2D

A flat architectural plan. Good for seeing the whole floor and for small screens.

3D

The full model with walls, doors and furniture. Switching between 2D and 3D keeps your route and floor.

Labels

Shows or hides the name pills on the map. Every room keeps a label when labels are on, and overlapping labels shift apart.

Camera

Reset view

Returns the camera to the default overview. The needle turns as you rotate the map.

Go to start point

Centers the map on where you are, which is the main reception unless you chose another start.

Zoom in and out

The plus and minus buttons step the zoom. The readout under them shows the current level, for example 2.9X.

Top bar

Kiosk Screen

Opens the touch-screen welcome page described under Kiosk mode. Shortcut: K.

Mobile view

Shows the app inside a phone frame, already walking a route, with the phone layout described below. Shortcut: M.

Refresh

Resets the demo: clears the route and selection, returns to the ground floor, empties the search and resets the camera. In the phone frame, the same icon restarts the walk.

Keyboard shortcuts

Opens the list of shortcuts. The ? key does the same from anywhere.

Demo Data

Browse and export the data behind the map. See Demo Data and export. Shortcut: D.

Room card

Click any room on the map or in the list.

Details

Name, code, floor, opening hours, live status, phone extension, whether it is step-free accessible, and the consultants who work there.

Instant Nav

A small QR block on the card for continuing the journey on a phone.

Navigate Here and Start From Here

Navigate Here makes a route from your start to this room and shows the walk time and distance. Start From Here moves your start point to this room.

Route planning

Once a destination is chosen, the search card turns into the route panel.

From, To and swap

Change the start from the drop-down, and use the swap button to reverse the journey for the walk back.

Step-Free (Lift)

On, the route uses lifts and avoids stairs. Off, it takes the fastest path and may use the Atrium Stairs. Flip it on the Willow Ward demo route to compare.

Walk time, distance, levels

Three figures at the top of the panel summarise the journey, and a chip shows the distance on each floor.

Turn-by-turn directions

Written steps with icons for turns, straight ahead, lifts and stairs, and arrival. Tap a step to jump the camera to it.

Simulate Walk and Phone QR

Simulate Walk starts the walkthrough. Phone QR opens the hand-off screen.

Walkthrough

The bar at the top centre steps through the route one instruction at a time.

Previous and next step

Jump back or forward by one instruction. The bar shows the current step, the metres remaining and what comes next.

Play and pause

Starts or pauses the walk. The speed button beside it switches between 1x and 2x. Floors change by themselves when the route takes a lift or stairs.

Follow camera

Puts the camera behind the walker, looking along the route, like walking it yourself. Dragging or scrolling the map switches it off.

Zoom to route

Pulls back so the whole route is in view.

QR and close

The QR icon opens the phone hand-off. closes the route and returns the camera to the overview.

Menu button

While a walk plays, the search card tucks away behind this three-line button at the top left so the map is clear. Press it to bring the card back.

What you see on the floor

The route is drawn as a train of chevrons. In each group the chevrons brighten toward the direction of travel. One arrow moves along the line. A red pin marks the destination.

Dark at the back, light at the front, so the route always reads as moving forward.

Phone view

A phone-sized layout that mirrors how the route looks on a visitor's own device.

Three stat pills

Steps left, arrival time, and distance remaining. The step count and time are estimates from the distance.

Upcoming-turn card

A small card appears near the top left only when a turn, lift or stairs is within about 30 m. It names the move and the distance.

Half-circle minimap

The whole floor in a dome at the bottom, with the route in blue, the destination in red, and the person-in-a-pin marker for you. The figure's legs swing while the walk plays.

Bottom controls

Speed toggle on the left, a large play and pause button in the middle, and Share on the right to open the QR screen.

Kiosk mode

For a touch screen in the lobby.

Welcome screen

A full-screen page headed Smart Wayfinding Kiosk · Main Lobby (G) with a grid of large destination cards, each showing the name, floor, code and status. Tapping a card closes the kiosk and shows the route from Main Reception. The Phone QR button on the route panel then carries it to a phone.

Step-Free Wheelchair Mode

A single switch on the page that limits routes to lifts and step-free paths.

Explore Interactive 2D / 3D Map

Leaves the welcome page and opens the full map. A language button on the page flips between English and Hindi.

Phone hand-off

Send the route to a phone

A screen with a QR block and a share link that holds the start, destination and step-free choice. It opens straight in the phone's browser with no app to install. Copy Shareable Route Link puts the link on the clipboard.

Demo Data and export

Demo Routes (6)

Six prepared journeys you can launch in one press, for example Car Park P1 → Maternity, ED Triage → Radiology X-Ray and Eye Centre (L1) → Pharmacy.

Departments and rooms (65)

The full list with codes, categories and floors. Inspect a room or start a route from here.

Doctor directory (14)

Consultants with their specialty, clinic and schedule.

Export

Copy or download the data as Wayfinding JSON (with the full routing graph), Indoor GeoJSON (RFC 7946) or CSV, so the same data can feed other systems.

Why it helps

Benefits

Patients and visitors

  • Fewer wrong turns. The route shows every corridor, door and floor change before the walk starts.
  • Written and visual. Directions come as a map line, as step text, and as a walkthrough, so anyone can use the form that suits them.
  • Their own language. English and Hindi, switched with one button.
  • Takes it along. A QR code or link continues the route on a phone.

Accessibility

  • Step-free routing. One switch swaps stairs for lifts, and every room says whether it is step-free accessible.
  • Kiosk wheelchair mode. Large controls and step-free routes only.
  • Compare both ways. Staff can show the lift route and the stairs route side by side.

Reception and staff

  • Fewer "where is…" questions. Visitors can answer themselves at a kiosk or on their phone.
  • One thing to show. Point at the screen instead of giving directions from memory.
  • Room cards carry hours, status and extension numbers in one place.

Hospital and product teams

  • One dataset drives everything. 2D, 3D, search, routing and exports read the same data, so they never disagree.
  • Open exports. JSON, GeoJSON and CSV for other tools.
  • Easy to show. One link or one file demonstrates the idea to a client.

Good to know

What the demo is, and is not

Demo data

The hospital, the doctors and the opening hours are made up for the demonstration.

QR codes

The QR blocks are a decorative pattern, so they will not scan yet. The copied share link works.

Position

"You are here" is the chosen start point. The demo does not track a real phone or beacon, and the walk is a simulation along the route.

Hindi and fonts

The Hindi wording has not been reviewed by a native speaker. Fonts load from Google, so offline the text falls back to system fonts.

Phone view

It is a preview frame inside the desktop page, so the layout can be shown without a phone.

Built with React, Vite, Tailwind CSS and three.js. Routes use a shortest-path search over a graph of corridors, doors, lifts and stairs, with a step-free option.
